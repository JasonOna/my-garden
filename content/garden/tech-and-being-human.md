# Tech & Being Human

**The raw tension:** Technology, especially AI, has real upside but also real potential for manipulation — especially when it comes to kids. Prompted partly by Australia's under-16 social media ban, which I fully agree with.

**The social media side:** A lot of what makes certain content go viral is manufactured intimacy — an attractive, provocative performer reciting other people's grievances (often men's rants) not because she believes them, but because that combination of attraction + emotional resonance reliably captures lonely attention. That attention gets monetized (often funneling to OnlyFans). It's emotionally compelling but hollow — fast food for connection. It doesn't push anyone to be better; it just feeds a craving.

**The AI side:** By contrast, AI — used well — can force intentionality. Riffing with an assistant, articulating half-formed thoughts, is itself a form of self-understanding. It prompts active reflection instead of passive consumption. (This conversation is basically proof of concept.)

**The sharper frame:** It's not really about AI vs. social media. It's **consumption vs. creation**. Social media is a consumption loop — scroll, receive, react. Riffing, writing, making — that's creation. The value of any technology might hinge on which direction it pulls you.

**The deeper layer:** This connects to something spiritual — "it is more blessed to give than to receive" (Acts 20:35). Giving and creating share a posture; receiving and consuming share another. As a culture — especially a capitalist one — we don't really promote creating. That might not be an accident. Capitalism profits more from consumption than creation, so the manipulation isn't just psychological, it's structural.

**Editorial note to self:** This can tip into feeling conspiratorial pretty fast. That's okay to sit with, but it's not where the piece should land. Don't end in doom or whining — push toward something inspirational and actionable. The eventual essay's job: show how to actually choose creating over consuming, giving over receiving, in ordinary life.
